# Writing strategies

How a writer approaches a persuasive text. The planner reads this to recognise the participant's
strategy from how they deliberated; the profile reads it to name the strategy they showed. A participant
can show more than one; one usually leads. Edit freely: the files in this folder are read on every call.

## draft-first
Wants to see a short draft early and shape it by reacting to it.
- Signals: asks for "a first version", talks about wording and length more than content, little planning.
- Scaffold: start from a compact full draft early; compare whole versions; clarify content by editing.

## arguments-first
Collects many arguments or points before deciding which to use.
- Signals: lists reasons, benefits, facts; "and also…"; little about order or tone.
- Scaffold: first put the arguments on the table (all of them, sharpened), then weed out and rank them;
  only then shape the text around the chosen ones.

## needs-first
Starts from the reader: who they are, what they need, what pressure they are under.
- Signals: talks about the recipient, their goals, worries and constraints; asks what they want.
- Scaffold: first clarify the reader's needs (which ones matter most), then how to respond to each.

## risk-first
Thinks about what could go wrong: objections, pushback, the worst case.
- Signals: "they might say…", "the problem is…", "we must not…", the competitor, the weak point.
- Scaffold: surface the objections first, decide how to handle each (address, pre-empt, leave out),
  then build the case around that.

## goal-first
Starts from the outcome they want: the ask, the decision, the next step.
- Signals: "the point is to get…", talks about the call to action, the deal, the target.
- Scaffold: fix the ask and the desired outcome first, then work backwards to what supports it.

## structure-first
Thinks in sections and order before content.
- Signals: "first…, then…, at the end…", talks about the opening, the order of points.
- Scaffold: settle the outline and order first, then fill each part.

## relationship-first
Puts the relationship and tone at the centre.
- Signals: talks about trust, the history with the customer, being friendly or firm, how it should feel.
- Scaffold: settle the stance and tone first, then fit the arguments to it.

## example-first
Works from a model: a past email, a template, how a colleague would do it.
- Signals: "usually I take…", "my colleague would…", "like the one we sent last year".
- Scaffold: start from a familiar, conventional shape and adapt it.

## undecided
Says little, or says many unconnected things without a clear approach.
- Scaffold: start with two clearly different whole approaches so the participant can react, and learn
  the strategy from their choices.
